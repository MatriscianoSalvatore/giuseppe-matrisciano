Who owns the knife? Segment by use, not by demographics.

I used to think marketing was mostly advertising. Then a final project in Consumer Analytics at the University of Georgia asked me to test a stereotype with real data.

The question

Do gender and age predict who owns non-kitchen knives? Most people would guess yes.

What the data said

Across 630 survey responses, men and women owned nearly the same number of knives, and age showed little correlation with ownership. I used descriptive statistics, t-tests, correlation analysis and multiple regression. Neither gender nor age was a significant predictor.

What I would do with it

People were buying knives for practical needs and hobbies, so traditional demographic segmentation was a poor predictor of behavior. I would segment by use, talk to the people who actually use the product (outdoor lovers, hobbyists and professionals), and create marketing that appeals to a broader range of consumers instead of relying on old stereotypes.

Assumptions should never replace evidence when making business decisions.

The data explains the consumer; creativity turns it into strategy.
