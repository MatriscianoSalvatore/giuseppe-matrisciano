AI will transform marketing. It won't replace marketers.

Three months after I landed in Florida, my first class presentation in English was about artificial intelligence in cars. Five years later, the same question keeps coming back in my marketing classes: what does AI change, and what does it leave to people?

Why I think this is the trend to watch

I believe artificial intelligence is the trend that will most transform marketing in the next five years. Companies are already using AI to better understand consumer behavior, improve the customer experience and create personalized advertising. It keeps developing every day, so I think it will become an even more important tool for making marketing decisions.

What AI is good at: speed

One big advantage of AI is that it can analyze large amounts of data much faster than people can. That helps companies identify trends, understand what customers want and respond more quickly to changes in consumer behavior. Faster analysis means faster and more informed decisions.

What it makes possible: personalization

I also believe AI will make marketing much more personalized. Instead of creating the same campaign for everyone, companies will be able to understand each customer's interests and preferences. That means more relevant content and a better customer experience.

What it can't do: the human part

However, I do not believe AI can replace people. Marketing is not just about data. Creativity, communication and understanding human emotions are still what build strong relationships with customers. I think AI should support people by providing insights, while people keep making the strategic and creative decisions.

What AI adds

  • Speed in analyzing data
  • Trends spotted sooner
  • Personalized content for each customer

What stays with people

  • Creativity
  • Communication
  • Understanding human emotions
  • The strategic decision

The best results will come from combining AI with human creativity.

What a statistics project taught me about tools

A project at the University of Georgia showed me why. I analyzed 630 survey responses to test a stereotype about who owns knives. The analysis explained the consumer, but data alone was not enough to solve the problem. I had to ask a meaningful question first, and then turn the result into recommendations a company could actually use. That second part needed creativity.

Read the post about that project

Where I want to use it

One of the reasons I chose the M.S. in Marketing at the University of Miami is its focus on the application of artificial intelligence in marketing. I want to learn how to combine AI with creativity and strategic thinking, to help companies understand their customers and adapt to an industry that changes quickly.

In five years I see myself as a Digital Marketing Manager, working with creative and analytics teams and using consumer insights and data to make better marketing decisions. AI will be part of that work, not a replacement for it.
